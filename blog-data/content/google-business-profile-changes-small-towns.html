For a lot of small-town businesses, your Google Business Profile is the actual first impression — before your website, before a phone call. It's the card someone sees when they search "plumber near me" or "coffee shop open now." When Google adjusts how these profiles work, it's worth a few minutes to check yours.

What tends to change

Google regularly adjusts three things that matter most to small local businesses: verification requirements (how you prove you actually run the business), which categories and attributes show up in search filters, and how heavily recent activity — photos, posts, review responses — factors into ranking.

What to check this month

Make sure your primary business category is still the most accurate one available (Google adds new categories over time). Confirm your hours are correct, especially around holidays. Add a recent photo — even a phone photo of your storefront or a finished project counts. And respond to any new reviews, good or bad; profiles with recent owner activity are treated more favorably.

Why this matters more in a small town

In a smaller market, there are fewer competitors, which means the businesses that stay active on their profile have an outsized advantage over the ones that set it up once and forgot about it. A ten-minute monthly check can be the difference between showing up first or fourth.
