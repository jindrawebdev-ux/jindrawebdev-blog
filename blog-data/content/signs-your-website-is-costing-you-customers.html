You don't need a redesign to know something's wrong. Usually it shows up as a feeling: fewer calls than you'd expect, people asking questions your website should already answer, or a gut sense that your site "looks fine" but isn't really working for you. Here are the five most common culprits, in order of how often they actually cost a sale.

1. It's slow — especially on a phone

Most visitors to a small, local business site are on their phone, often standing in a parking lot or scrolling on a break. If your homepage takes more than a few seconds to load, a real percentage of those people are gone before they see anything you offer. Page speed isn't a vanity metric — it's the first filter your website has to pass.

2. It wasn't built mobile-first

A site that was designed for a desktop screen and then "shrunk down" almost always has some awkward moment on mobile: text too small to read, buttons too close together, a menu that's hard to find. If it takes more than one thumb-tap to figure out what to do next, you're losing people.

3. There's no clear next step

Every page should answer one question for the visitor: what do I do now? Call? Fill out a form? Book a time? If your homepage has five equally-loud buttons — or none — visitors leave without doing anything, even if they liked what they saw.

4. Contact info is buried

This sounds obvious, but it's one of the most common issues on small business sites. Phone number and email should be visible without hunting, ideally in the header and footer of every page, not just tucked into a contact page three clicks deep.

5. It hasn't been touched in years

An outdated site — old photos, an expired promotion, a copyright year from three years ago — quietly signals "we might not be around" even for businesses that are thriving. It doesn't need to be flashy. It just needs to look cared for.

What to fix first

If you only have time for one thing, start with mobile speed and a single clear call to action above the fold. Those two changes tend to have the biggest, fastest impact on how many visitors actually reach out.
